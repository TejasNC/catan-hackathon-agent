How comboop1 plays Catan

comboop1 is two bots merged. The mid-game comes from combo: the v5 value function, regime-B weights and a within-turn turn-plan lookahead (cop1_base.py, cop1_core.py). The opening comes from op1: a learned opening model plus a draft search (cop1_opener.py, open_model.json, and cop1_mbase.py, plain v5, used to predict the opponent).

1Overview

  1. Sample a world. The opponent's hand and the dev-card pile are hidden, so the bot draws one full game state consistent with what it can see.
  2. Simulate every legal action on a copy of that world.
  3. Score each result with a hand-built value function \(V\), measured in victory points. Random outcomes are averaged, not sampled.
  4. Look a little further for actions that pay off later: trade → build, knight → robber, road building → 2 roads. In the main phase, the top candidates are played out greedily to the end of the turn.
  5. Play the best score. A turn-plan replaces the 1-ply choice only if it wins by a margin.

Budget. The clock allows about 300 ms + 12.5 ms per decision, roughly 1 s of CPU per game. One copy-and-execute costs about 0.1 ms and one \(V\) call about 0.1 ms, so a decision affords 60–100 simulated actions. A random playout costs 11 ms, which rules out MCTS. What fits is a good evaluation with depth 1–2 search.

2The value function

\[ V(s) = \mathrm{Score}_{\text{me}}(s) - k\,\mathrm{Score}_{\text{opp}}(s) + w_{\text{army}}\,\mathrm{clip}(K_{\text{me}}-K_{\text{opp}},\pm 2) + \tfrac{1}{2}\,w_{\text{road}}\,\mathrm{clip}(L_{\text{me}}-L_{\text{opp}},\pm 2) \]

Here \(k = 0.737\), \(w_{\text{army}} = 0.20\), \(w_{\text{road}} = 0.26\). \(K\) is knights played and \(L\) is longest-road length.

Every term is in VP units, so each weight reads as a claim: "a ready-to-build city hand is worth 0.47 VP". Two consequences:

  • Weights can be sanity-checked against the game. A settlement is +1 VP plus its production, so a hand term above 1 VP is absurd. That check exposed the learned-eval failure in §8.
  • \(k < 1\) means race, don't block. Costing the opponent 1 VP is worth 0.74 of gaining 1 VP.

Production fades with the game's phase:

\[ \text{phase} = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{\text{lead VP} - 2}{7},\,0,\,1\right), \qquad w_p = 0.055\,(1-\text{phase}) + 0.0495\,\text{phase} \]

3The regime φ: expand early, build late

Each player gets \(\varphi \in [0,1]\), computed once per decision at the root and then held fixed:

\[ \varphi = \max\!\left( \underbrace{\mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{\text{VP} - 4}{4}\right)}_{\text{score}},\; \underbrace{1 - \min\!\left(1,\ \frac{\text{best open spot pips}}{10}\right)}_{\text{space}} \right) \]

The space part is 1 when no settlements remain. "Best open spot" means reachable with at most one road. Regime B uses \(a = 0.5\), fade 0.35 and dev 0.25, and changes three things:

Resource weights

\[ q = \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}\varphi, \qquad \rho_r \leftarrow \rho_r\left(1 + a\left[(1-q)\,E_r + q\,B_r - 1\right]\right) \] \[ E = (1.25,\ 1.25,\ 1,\ 0.9,\ 0.6), \qquad B = (0.6,\ 0.6,\ 0.9,\ 1.35,\ 1.55) \]

At \(\varphi = 1\): wood and brick ×0.80, sheep ×0.95, wheat ×1.18, ore ×1.28.

Fade expansion, raise dev cards

\[ \text{fade} = 1 - 0.35\,\varphi, \qquad m_d = 1 + 0.25\,\varphi \]

4Decision procedure

1. One-ply scoring

Every legal action \(a\) is executed on a copy of the sampled world and scored \(V(s_a)\).

2. Chance nodes in expectation

Buying a dev card. The sampled draw is replaced by the mean over the unseen pile, about 0.30 VP for a fresh pile:

\[ \mathbb{E}[\text{dev}] = \frac{0.095\,m_d\,n_K + 1\cdot n_{VP} + 0.18\,m_d\,n_{\text{other}}}{n} \]

Robber steal. Scored as \(V + 0.061\,(1+k)\): one card for me, one fewer for them.

Without this, every dev-card decision would be a coin flip on one sample.

3. Two-step follow-ups

  • Trade, Year of Plenty, Monopoly: scored as the best city, settlement, dev card or road reachable right afterwards. This covers the top 6 such actions, with up to 12 follow-ups each.
  • Knight: includes the greedy robber placement it triggers.
  • Road Building: includes the two greedy free roads.

4. Turn-plan lookahead

This runs only in the main phase of a turn.

  • Take the top 3 first actions by score, plus the base choice from steps 1–3.
  • Extend each greedily, up to 6 steps, until ending the turn scores best.
  • Score the end-of-turn state, keeping the chance corrections from step 2.
  • Switch from the base choice only if a plan beats it by 0.05 VP. The margin keeps search noise from overriding the evaluation.

Data. 16,000 v5-vs-v5 games. Clock. Each search loop stops at \(\min\big(150\ \mathrm{ms},\ (\text{CPU left} - 80\ \mathrm{ms})/2\big)\). Below 120 ms the search is skipped, and any failure falls back to combo's own draft.

6How the weights were obtained, and the results

  1. Hand-set weights for v1–v3 (INSIGHTS §8).
  2. SPSA self-play. Weights live in log space, \(\theta_k = \theta_k^{0}\,e^{x_k}\). Each iteration draws \(\Delta \in \{\pm 1\}^d\) and plays \(\theta^{\pm} = x \pm 0.25\,\Delta\) against each other on 64 paired games. With \(s\) = the win rate of \(\theta^{+}\):
    \[ x \leftarrow x + a_t\,(2s - 1)\,\frac{\Delta}{2}, \qquad a_t = \frac{0.6}{(1 + t/100)^{0.602}} \]
    93 iterations gave v5. F was never an opponent or a teacher; it is only a held-out measuring stick.
  3. Regime B (\(a = 0.5\), fade 0.35, dev 0.25) was set by hand. An SPSA run with the regime on landed at 0.77 / 0.26 / 0.24 and was no better (0.503 vs v5).

All results are win rates. The 95 % interval is \(\pm 1.96\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}\), about ±0.025 at \(n = 1000\).

7Worked example

The position is hypothetical; the weights and formulas are the bot's real ones.

Situation. I have 5 VP and the opponent has 5 public VP, so \(\text{phase} = 3/7\) and \(w_p = 0.0526\). My best open spot has 9 pips, so \(\varphi = \max(0.25,\ 0.10) = 0.25\), and the regime-adjusted weights are \(\rho \approx (0.98,\ 1.20,\ 1.11,\ 1.75,\ 1.73)\).

That is 8 cards, so a 7 would cost 4 of them. I own a 3:1 port. The table shows only the terms that change; the opponent's score is unchanged.

  • Hand deltas net out the discard risk. For the city: −0.30 card count, +0.71 for escaping the 4-card discard, −0.47 ready-city lost, −0.12 ready-dev lost, −0.02 partial.
  • The follow-up step matters. Alone the trade is worth +0.29; with the settlement after it, +1.31. That is how a 1-ply bot plans trade → build.
  • The city wins on wheat and ore production, which the regime boosts as φ grows. The dev card's 0.30 VP can't compete with 1 VP plus production.
  • The turn-plan adds nothing here. After the city the hand is wood 1, sheep 1, wheat 1, and nothing more is affordable, so the bot builds the city.

8Known limits and what did not work

  • A learned linear eval is a trap. Logistic regression on outcomes predicts the winner well (78 % accuracy) but wins only 10–19 % as a policy. It values holding city cards above building the city, so the bot hoards. Features that predict winning are not features worth choosing, and fitting on after-states did not fix it.
  • Search on top of a tuned eval adds little. Opponent-roll expectimax at end of turn scored 0.42–0.46; robber lookahead 0.498; danger mode 0.497; a second SPSA round 0.503; 1v1 community heuristics 0.47–0.51. Once SPSA has tuned the weights, small structural changes get absorbed.
  • Opening limits. Roads are ignored when an opening is scored. The opponent model is v5's draft, not F's "pips + 4 per resource". Training data is v5 vs v5 only.
  • One sampled world. Late-game hidden VP-card risk is not averaged.
  • The next real gain needs a different model class, such as TD(λ) on after-states.
